Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

Steep + Drain
  1. 1. Pre-infusion
    At 0:00 · Pour 30g · up to 30g · 40s
    First rinse the paper filter with hot water and preheat the dripper (rinse with the valve open, then discard the water) — immersion brewers are usually resin, a cold body steals heat, and you are about to steep for three minutes, so this matters more here than in percolation.
    Close the bottom valve (Clever Dripper / Hario Switch) and pour in 2x the dose of 93°C water, covering all the grounds, then swirl the dripper gently so every dry particle is wetted. Wait about 30 seconds for CO2 to escape.
    ℹ️ With the valve shut this is not strictly a "bloom" (that is a percolation idea) — the right word is pre-infusion: degas the bed and wet it evenly.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 4-4.5 (texture like coarse sugar, on the fine side)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~24-27 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.4-2.7 turns
  2. 2. Fill with remaining water
    At 0:40 · Pour 195g · up to 225g · 33s
    93°C, pour in the remaining water over about 33 seconds. Speed genuinely does not matter — the valve is closed, so nothing escapes and pour technique has almost no effect here. After pouring, give one gentle stir (or another swirl) so no clumps float on top.
  3. 3. Steep for 2:55
    At 1:13 · holding at 225g · 175s
    Cover with a lid to keep warm (optional). The grounds steep fully in the water - no action needed. This full contact makes the extraction even and stable.
  4. 4. Open valve and drain
    At 4:08 · holding at 225g · 60s
    Open the valve and let the coffee drain naturally (about 60 seconds). Remove the dripper once drained and enjoy. Note: do not shake or press mid-drain - let it flow on its own.

Full method guide

Origin

Immersion pour-over is one of the two core brewing principles alongside percolation, represented by the Clever Dripper and the Hario Switch. Closing the bottom valve lets the grounds steep fully in hot water - no pouring technique required, and the most even extraction.

Core idea

The core of immersion is letting the grounds sit fully submerged in water - unlike percolation, which relies on pouring technique to control extraction, immersion gives coffee and water a fixed contact time, so results are highly repeatable. Ideal for beginners building a stable baseline, and for objectively comparing flavor differences between beans.

Key points

  • Setup: rinse the paper filter with hot water and preheat the dripper (rinse with the valve open, then discard) — the resin body steals heat and you are steeping for three minutes, so this matters more than in percolation
  • The key is pouring with the valve closed: no water escapes, so pour speed does not matter
  • Pre-infusion: with the valve closed, pour 2x the dose and swirl the dripper gently so every dry particle is wetted, then wait about 30 seconds to degas — with the valve shut it is not a "bloom", which is a percolation idea
  • Then fill in the remaining water and stir once gently so no clumps float on top
  • Rest for about 3 minutes (the site scales this to 2:30-4:00 by dose; no action needed while steeping). Put a lid or a small plate on top — three minutes costs you 4-8°C, and since the water is already in the dripper there is no kettle to reheat
  • Open the bottom valve and let the coffee drain naturally - no pressing or shaking
  • Two schools on grind: one step coarser at 93°C (this site's choice, for the longer contact time) or medium-fine at 90°C (Hoffmann's Clever recipe, water in before coffee); drawdown after opening the valve takes about 60-90 seconds — coarser grinds and coffee-first setups drain slower

Beans it suits

Most consistent with medium roasts; ideal for tasting a bean purely on its own merits, since the extraction variables are easiest to control

Less suited to

Situations calling for complex layering or fine acidity control (Kasuya or Winton fit better)

FAQ

What if I have no Clever Dripper or Hario Switch
The closest substitute is a French press (also full immersion, though the metal mesh leaves fines and oils for a heavier body). If you want immersion cleanliness, steep in a mug or measuring jug and then pour it through a paper filter — the result is nearly identical. Do not plug the bottom of a V60 with a finger or a stopper: a cone will not seal, and this is 93°C water
Too bitter
Shorten the steep (to 2.5 minutes) or grind half a step coarser
Too sour / too weak
Extend the steep to 3.5 minutes or grind half a step finer
Tastes different every time
Immersion is among the most consistent methods; big swings usually mean the grinder setting was not locked in properly
