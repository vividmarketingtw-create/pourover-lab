Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee（15g / 248g）

Three Pours · Rao Spin x3
  1. 1. Bloom + aggressive Rao Spin
    At 0:00 · Pour 45g · 40s
    Rinse the paper filter with hot water and preheat the dripper (Rao prefers a plastic V60 — better heat retention than glass or ceramic). Discard the water, add the grounds, and press a bird's-nest well into the center of the dry bed with a finger before starting the timer.
    Pour 3x the dose (45g) of 97°C water, then spin the dripper aggressively straight away (the Rao Spin) to level the bed and break up clumps. The bloom is fixed at 40 seconds (the original value; it does not scale with dose).
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 3.5-4 (texture like fine sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~21-24 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.1-2.4 turns
  2. 2. Second pour (up to 150g)
    At 0:40 · Pour 105g · 26s
    97°C. Keep the spout low, the stream vertical and the height constant, spiralling steadily from the center outward for 105g in about 26 seconds (about 4 g/s). Finish with a gentle Rao Spin lasting under one second to wash the wall grounds back into the bed.
  3. 3. Wait until about 70% drained
    At 1:06 · 15s
    Watch the dripper here, not the clock — Rao's trigger is the slurry dropping by roughly 70% before the next pour. The site converts that to about 15 seconds with the V60 drawdown model (it scales with dose, grind and your 'My flow' setting), but go by what you actually see.
    If your dripper always clears much faster than 15s, move 'My flow' toward fast; if slower, toward slow.
  4. 4. Third pour (up to 248g)
    At 1:21 · Pour 98g · 25s
    97°C, same low steady spiral, adding the remaining 98g in about 25 seconds (about 3.9 g/s).
  5. 5. Final Rao Spin + drawdown
    At 1:46 · 30s
    Immediately give one more gentle Rao Spin to level the bed — this is the whole point of the technique, an uneven bed channels. Then do not touch it again; let it drain, about 2:16 overall.
    💡 Rao has published totals between 3:00 and 4:30 (he grinds finer than a typical V60 and targets 22-24.5% extraction). To move toward his high-extraction end: grind half a step finer, or move 'My flow' toward slow.

Full method guide

Origin

Proposed by American coffee expert Scott Rao, famous for the Rao Spin swirling technique, emphasizing even extraction and high extraction yield. This site follows the three-pour version published on the Hario ambassador page: 20g / 330g (1:16.5), 97°C, plastic V60. Rao has also demonstrated a one-pour 'cafe version' (faster and simpler); the staged version is the one he uses to chase maximum extraction. Both are his — this site uses the staged one. Rao argues for water at or near boiling (he considers 97-98°C, even straight off the boil, entirely safe — you are not "burning" anything).

Core idea

Use the Rao Spin (swirling the dripper) to level the bed, paired with a steady low spiral pour, for even extraction, a high extraction yield (he targets 22-24.5%) and a layered, weighty mouthfeel. The key is three Rao Spins: one hard spin after the bloom to break up clumps, then a gentle one after each pour so the bed never sits uneven — an uneven bed channels.

Key points

  • Original reference: 20g / 330g (1:16.5), 97°C, plastic V60 (better heat retention than glass or ceramic)
  • Setup: rinse the paper filter and preheat the dripper, discard the water, add the grounds and press a bird's-nest well into the center of the dry bed before starting the timer
  • Bloom: pour 3x the dose (20g becomes 60g), then immediately spin the dripper hard to break clumps and level the bed
  • The bloom is fixed at 40 seconds and does not scale with dose
  • Second pour: start at 0:40 and go to about 60% of total water (200g at 20g dose). Keep the spout low, the stream vertical, the height constant. Finish with a sub-second gentle Rao Spin
  • Wait until the bed has drained about 70% (watch the water level, not the clock), then pour the third stage to 100% and give one more gentle Rao Spin
  • Then do not touch it. Rao has published totals between 3:00 and 4:30 (he grinds finer than a typical V60 and targets 22-24.5% extraction); the times shown here are computed by the drawdown model
  • Use 20-22g or more — Rao argues that small doses force an overly fine grind, which raises bitterness and lowers extraction

Beans it suits

Dense light roasts benefit most — the agitation from three Rao Spins is exactly what pulls a hard-to-extract light roast up to 22-24.5%. Medium roasts also suit it well and come out balanced and rich. Overall it suits advanced brewers chasing extraction yield, and anyone brewing large doses (he insists on 20-22g minimum)

Less suited to

Dark roasts (loose structure plus hot water and heavy agitation over-extract into bitterness easily — if you must, drop below 90°C and spin more gently); and any time you just want something simple, since the force and timing of the spins make or break this method

FAQ

Too bitter/astringent
Grind half a step coarser first — this method agitates hard and already runs a high yield, so grind is the first lever when it over-extracts. After that, soften the final Rao Spin or lower the spout further
Too sour / under-extracted
Grind half a step finer, and check that the post-bloom Rao Spin is genuinely hard — spinning until the bed is completely flat. Under-doing that one spin is the most common cause of under-extraction
Total time too long
Grind is too fine or there are too many fines — go half a step coarser. If it is always long, move 'My flow' toward slow (it also lengthens the drain-wait in step 3)
Total time too short
Grind is too coarse — go finer. Also check you really waited until roughly 70% drained before the third pour
Muddled flavor
Keep the spiral consistent - no speeding up and slowing down. Low spout, vertical stream, constant height are the three things Rao stresses
When exactly do I start the third pour?
Watch the dripper — once the slurry above the bed has dropped by roughly 70%. The seconds on the timer are only a converted estimate
