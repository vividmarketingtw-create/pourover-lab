Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 251g)

Bloom + Continuous Centre Pour
  1. 1. Bloom (fixed 30 seconds)
    At 0:00 · Pour 30g · up to 30g · 30s
    Rinse the paper filter and the dripper with hot water first — Wang stresses this step; he says an unrinsed filter strips the juicy aromatics from the coffee. The original uses a ceramic V60 with a white filter.
    Pour 2x the dose (30g) at 93°C, circling gently from the centre so every particle is wetted, then wait until the 30-second mark before the main pour (the original value; it does not scale with dose).
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 3.5-4 (texture like fine sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~21-24 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.1-2.4 turns
    ℹ️ The competition original is finer still — most of it sits around 400um after sifting (roughly Feima 2.5-3 / Comandante 13-16). You can go there for a full replica, but not above about 25g: a deeper bed plus that grind stalls easily and the total time runs away.
  2. 2. Continuous centre pour (up to 251g)
    At 0:30 · Pour 221g · up to 251g · 44s
    93°C. From 0:30, pour without ever breaking the stream, straight into the centre of the bed — no spiral, no stages — adding the remaining 221g in about 44 seconds (about 5 g/s).
    A centre pour keeps the water level above the bed the whole time; that is what people mean by 'maintaining the water level'. It is a consequence of the continuous pour, not a separate move.
    💡 The original 15g / 250g pour window is 40-45 seconds. The stream must be decisive and steady — wobble shows up directly in the cup.
  3. 3. Let it drain
    At 1:14 · holding at 251g · 35s
    No stirring, no swirling — just let it drain, about 1:49 overall.
    💡 The original 15g / 250g finishes draining at 2:00-2:15 (another source records 15g / 300g at 92°C finishing 2:05-2:10 — the water amount differs, the pour structure does not). It is one of the shortest methods on the site: a very fine grind plus a strong centre pour maxes out extraction in a short window.

Full method guide

Origin

The 2017 World Brewers Cup winning recipe of Taiwanese barista Chad Wang — Taiwan's first WBrC title. This site uses the competition recipe: 15g / 250g (1:16.7), 93°C, ceramic V60 with a white paper filter, ground very fine (most of it around 400um after sifting). Bloom with 30g, then from 0:30 pour continuously into the centre up to 250g over 40-45 seconds, draining by 2:00-2:15. (Another source records 15g / 300g at 1:20 and 92°C, finishing 2:05-2:10 — the water amount differs, the pour structure does not.)

Core idea

The core is a very fine grind plus an unbroken centre pour. The stream goes straight into the middle of the bed and never stops, so the water level stays above the grounds the whole way — what people call 'maintaining the water level' is a consequence of that, not a separate move. The fine grind supplies extraction efficiency and the continuous pour supplies steady extraction pressure; together they max out extraction in a little over two minutes, giving a clean, bright, focused cup.

Key points

  • Setup: rinse the filter and dripper with hot water — Wang stresses this; an unrinsed paper strips the juicy aromatics
  • Gear: ceramic V60 with a white filter (ceramic holds heat better)
  • 93°C held throughout (another source records 92°C)
  • Bloom: pour 2x the dose and wait until exactly 0:30
  • Main pour: from 0:30, pour without breaking the stream, straight into the centre — no spiral, no stages — finishing in about 40-45 seconds
  • The water level staying above the bed is a natural result of the centre pour; you do not manage it separately
  • Grind: the competition original sits mostly around 400um (very fine). This site defaults to a compromise medium-fine (Feima 3.5-4) because 400um stalls easily in the deeper beds of larger doses
  • The original 15g / 250g drains by 2:00-2:15, one of the shortest methods on the site

Beans it suits

Washed light roasts (florals and fruit acidity come through most completely); East African beans such as Ethiopia and Kenya are especially well suited

Less suited to

Dark roasts (a fine grind plus a strong continuous pour over-extracts them into bitterness)

FAQ

Too sour / under-extracted
Grind half a step finer (toward the original 400um) — this method runs on the fine grind; no amount of pouring makes up for grinding too coarse. After that, raise the water to 94°C
Too bitter / drying
Grind half a step coarser, or drop the water to 90-91°C. Extraction is already strong here, so grind is the first lever
Total time much longer than 2:15
The grind is too fine for the dose (or there are too many fines) and the bed has stalled — go half a step coarser. Above about 25g, do not push toward 400um
What if my stream breaks
Just pick it back up quickly. A break lets the level drop to the bed and releases the extraction pressure, which flattens the cup — this is why a kettle you can fill in one go helps
How does this differ from Single Pour
Structurally they are close (bloom, then one pour), but three things differ: Wang grinds noticeably finer, pours into the centre without spiralling, and pours faster (about 5 g/s vs 3.5 g/s). The result is a much shorter total and a more focused, brighter cup
