Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 215g)

Four equal pours · cool-start two temperatures
  1. 1. Pour 1: 54g (75°C) · Cool start
    At 0:00 · Pour 54g · up to 54g · 30s
    Pour the first 54g in circles with 75°C water (~12s). The cool start is the soul of this recipe — it wets the bed gently, locks in floral-fruity aromatics, and keeps fines from over-extracting into astringency at high heat.
    Two-temperature prep at home: heat water to 95°C, pour a small cup into another vessel and let it cool to ~75°C for this pour (a variable-temp kettle makes it easy).
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 4.5-5 (like raw sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~28-30 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.8-3.0 turns
    ℹ️ Competition original: 14g/200g (1:14), flat-bottom brewer, dual grind (75% 1000µm + 25% 800µm), 50g every 30 seconds for four pours, ~2:00 total, ~155g in the cup. A single medium-coarse grind is fine at home; Kalita Wave / Origami with a wave filter is closest to the original — a V60 works too (brighter cup).
  2. 2. Pour 2: 54g (95°C) · Hot kettle
    At 0:30 · Pour 54g · up to 108g · 30s
    Switch to the 95°C kettle. Pour 2: 54g (~12s), still in circles. After the cool start, the heat pulls out sweetness and tropical-fruit notes — aromatics first, sweetness second.
  3. 3. Pour 3: 54g (95°C) · Centre pour
    At 1:00 · Pour 54g · up to 162g · 30s
    Stay at 95°C. Pour 3: 54g (~12s) — switch to a steady centre pour: keep the stream on the middle of the bed, no more circles, so the late extraction stays clean and focused.
  4. 4. Pour 4: 53g (95°C)
    At 1:30 · Pour 53g · up to 215g · 12s
    95°C, final pour 53g (~12s), still on the centre. Then hands off — let it draw down.
  5. 5. Wait for drawdown
    At 1:42 · holding at 215g · 15s
    Let it finish dripping — about 1:57 overall.
    💡 The original 14g/200g finishes around 2:00 in a flat-bottom brewer. Too slow/astringent → grind half a step coarser; too fast/sour → half a step finer.

Full method guide

Origin

The 2022 World Brewers Cup champion recipe of Taiwanese barista Sherry Hsu (徐詩媛) — Taiwan’s second WBrC title (Melbourne). Competition original: 14g/200g (1:14), flat-bottom brewer, dual grind (75% 1000µm + 25% 800µm), first pour at 75°C and the rest at 95°C, 50g every 30 seconds for four pours, ~2:00 total, ~155g in the cup.

Core idea

Reverse two-temperature brewing: typical two-temp recipes (like Winton’s) start hot and finish cool; Sherry flips it — a 75°C cool start locks in floral aromatics and keeps fines from early over-extraction, then 95°C pulls out full sweetness and body. The pour pattern splits too: circles for the first two pours to open the bed, steady centre pours for the last two to finish clean.

Key points

  • Pour 1 at 75°C in circles: gentle wetting locks in volatile aromatics (and handles degassing)
  • From pour 2, switch to 95°C: heat draws out sweetness and tropical-fruit notes, still in circles
  • Pours 3-4 switch to centre pours: keep the stream on the middle so the late extraction stays clean
  • One pour every 30 seconds, four equal pours — a fixed rhythm, ~2:00-2:40 total
  • Original dual grind: 75% at 1000µm (citrus acidity) + 25% at 800µm (tropical sweetness); one medium-coarse grind is fine at home
  • A flat-bottom brewer (Kalita Wave / Origami with a wave filter) extracts more evenly and is the original choice; a V60 works too, with a brighter cup

Beans it suits

Light and medium-light floral-fruity coffees (Geisha, washed Ethiopians, CO₂-maceration and other special processes shine)

Less suited to

Dark roasts — the cool start adds little, and the 95°C phase easily over-extracts into bitterness

FAQ

Do I need a flat-bottom brewer?
The original uses one (more even extraction, steadier rhythm). A V60 works as well — brighter cup, slightly shorter total time.
I can’t do a dual grind at home
A single medium-coarse grind is fine (the recipe’s core is 1000µm). For an advanced version, grind 3/4 coarse + 1/4 fine and mix.
How do I get 75°C water?
Boil to 95°C, pour a small cup aside for 3-4 minutes (or add a splash of room-temperature water) and it lands near 75°C. A variable-temperature kettle is easiest.
