Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

5-Pour Method · Standard · pour every 45s
  1. 1. Pour 1: bloom (first 40% sets sweet/acid)
    At 0:00 · Pour 45g · up to 45g · 45s
    Pour 92-93°C water in a spiral from the center outward, about 7 seconds (be decisive, roughly 6.4 g/s — do not trickle); this pour doubles as the bloom and degassing.
    ⏱ 4:6 runs on a fixed cadence: start a pour every 45s (Kasuya's original value — it does not move with dose, roast or your flow setting), so you have about 38s before the next one — you do NOT wait for the bed to drain dry (Kasuya's method watches the clock, not the dripper).
    💡 Tuning: pour 1 bigger than pour 2 → brighter, more acidic; roughly equal → original balance; pour 1 smaller than pour 2 → sweeter, rounder

    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 4.5-5 (texture like coarse sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~28-30 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.8-3.0 turns
  2. 2. Pour 2 (first 40% sets sweet/acid)
    At 0:45 · Pour 45g · up to 90g · 45s
    92-93°C, start at 0:45, about 7 seconds. Pours 1 and 2 together make up 40% of the total water; their split sets the sweet-acid direction — currently set to "Balanced" (45g → 45g). Next pour starts 38s later; it is fine if water is still draining.
  3. 3. Pour 3 (last 60% controls strength)
    At 1:30 · Pour 45g · up to 135g · 45s
    92-93°C, start at 1:30, about 7 seconds. The last 3 pours total 60%: the standard 3 pours give balanced strength (= Kasuya's original recipe). Keep the 45s cadence.
  4. 4. Pour 4 (last 60% controls strength)
    At 2:15 · Pour 45g · up to 180g · 45s
    92-93°C, start at 2:15, about 7 seconds. The last 3 pours total 60%: the standard 3 pours give balanced strength (= Kasuya's original recipe). Keep the 45s cadence.
  5. 5. Pour 5 (last 60% controls strength)
    At 3:00 · Pour 45g · up to 225g · 7s
    92-93°C, start at 3:00, about 7 seconds. The last 3 pours total 60%: the standard 3 pours give balanced strength (= Kasuya's original recipe). This is the last pour — now just wait for drawdown.
  6. 6. Let it drain
    At 3:07 · holding at 225g · 15s
    Wait until the coffee has fully drained, then remove the dripper and enjoy.
    💡 Target total for this volume is about 3:22 (5 pours × 45s + ~15s drawdown). Kasuya's original benchmark for 20g/300g, balanced, standard strength is 3:30-3:45 (video-version cadence; Philocoffea's current official table moves the last pour up to 2:45).
    · If the bed drains dry well before each next pour and the cup tastes bitter or drying → grind half a step finer so water dwells longer in the bed.
    · If it is still dripping 15+ seconds past target → grind half a step coarser.
    · Never stretch the waits to hit the target time — in 4:6 the total is a result of the cadence, not the goal.

Full method guide

Origin

Invented by Japanese barista Tetsu Kasuya, the 2016 World Brewers Cup champion recipe. Recommended water 92-93°C, ground slightly coarser than a typical V60. His video version pours every 45 seconds with the last pour at 3:00 (kettle off 3:30-3:45); Philocoffea's current official table moves the last pour up to 2:45 and adjusts water temperature by roast level (83-93°C) — this site follows the video cadence.

Core idea

The core is the 4:6 rule - split the total water into a first 40% and a last 60%. The first pour of the 40% doubles as bloom and degassing and sets the sweet-acid balance: decrease the first pour for more sweetness, increase it for more acidity. The last 60% is split evenly and sets the strength — this site offers 2 / 3 / 4 pours (more pours, stronger; Kasuya's original is 1-3, and 4 is our extension). The cadence is one pour every 45 seconds: with a coarse enough grind the bed drains almost dry on its own before the next pour — watch the timer, not the dripper.

Key points

  • Grind noticeably coarser than a typical V60 (coarse sugar texture, Feima 600N setting 4.5-5 / Comandante ~28-30 clicks) - a distinctive, important setting of this method
  • Start a pour every 45 seconds by the clock; with a coarse enough grind the bed drains close to dry on its own before the next pour
  • Pour in a spiral from the center outward, avoiding pouring directly on the filter
  • The first two pours (40%) are the flavor key - their split sets the sweet-acid balance
  • The last 60% is split evenly; the number of pours sets the strength — more pours, stronger. The buttons here offer 2 / 3 / 4 (the original is 1-3; the standard 3 is the original recipe)

Beans it suits

Natural light roasts best showcase the precise control of this method; light-to-medium roasts of any process work well

Less suited to

Dark roasts (waiting out each drawdown tends to over-extract into bitterness)

FAQ

Too sour
Reduce the first pour to lift the sweetness
Too bitter
Grind one step coarser or drop the temperature 2°C
Total time runs well past target
Grind is too fine - go coarser
Inconsistent flavor
Keep the 45-second cadence steady and weigh every pour; if the bed is still flooded when the next pour is due, grind coarser
