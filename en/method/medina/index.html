Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 240g)

Five equal pours
  1. 1. Pour 1 (bloom)
    At 0:00 · Pour 48g · up to 48g · 30s
    91°C — pour 48g (~12s); the first pour doubles as the bloom. The pour path is a spiral outward and then back in (the original wording: spiraling out and in). Pours start at a fixed 0:00 / 0:30 / 1:00 / 1:30 / 2:00, unaffected by dose or your flow setting. Gentle low-temp extraction is the core of this method — round sweetness, no sharp edges.
    The champion used an Origami Air S with a conical filter; a V60 works the same. Grind: Feima 600N setting 4-4.5
    Other grinders: Comandante ~24-27 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.4-2.7 turns · the champion used a 1Zpresso ZP6
    ℹ️ Competition original: 15.5g / 250g (1:16) at 91°C on an Origami Air S with a conical filter, ground on a 1Zpresso ZP6, five equal 50g pours every 30 seconds (0:00 / 0:30 / 1:00 / 1:30 / 2:00), spiralling out and in, with a gentle stir at the end. Total 2:40-2:45.
  2. 2. Pour 2
    At 0:30 · Pour 48g · up to 96g · 30s
    91°C, pour 48g every 30s (~12s each), again spiralling out and back in. Fixed rhythm, repeated motion — consistency is the whole technique
  3. 3. Pour 3
    At 1:00 · Pour 48g · up to 144g · 30s
    91°C, pour 48g every 30s (~12s each), again spiralling out and back in. Fixed rhythm, repeated motion — consistency is the whole technique
  4. 4. Pour 4
    At 1:30 · Pour 48g · up to 192g · 30s
    91°C, pour 48g every 30s (~12s each), again spiralling out and back in. Fixed rhythm, repeated motion — consistency is the whole technique
  5. 5. Pour 5 (final)
    At 2:00 · Pour 48g · up to 240g · 12s
    After the final 48g, a gentle single stir helps integrate flavors (Medina's touch). Let it draw down — total ~2:32
  6. 6. Wait for drawdown
    At After pours · holding at 240g · 20s
    Just let it finish. This method is famous for repeatability — a great daily driver

Full method guide

Origin

Venezuelan-born barista Carlos Medina (competing for Chile) won the 2023 World Brewers Cup with this recipe, brewed on an Origami Air S with a conical filter.

Core idea

The core is extreme repeatability: five perfectly equal pours, a fixed 30-second interval, and gentle 91°C water — lock every variable down and every cup comes out the same. The lower temperature softens sharp acidity and bitterness, foregrounding round sweetness and balance.

Key points

  • Split total water into five equal pours, a fixed 30 seconds apart (locked here — it does not move with dose or flow setting): 0:00 / 0:30 / 1:00 / 1:30 / 2:00
  • 91°C — 2-3°C lower than most recipes, softening acidity
  • Steady spirals for every pour — outward and then back in (the original wording: spiraling out and in)
  • A gentle single stir after the last pour helps integration
  • The original 15.5g / 250g runs 2:40-2:45; the site recalculates from your dose and flow setting

Beans it suits

Medium and medium-light roasts; naturals and honeys get especially round sweetness at low temperature

Less suited to

Bright competition light roasts where you want acidity to pop (try the Wölfl fast-flow)

FAQ

How is this different from Winton's five-pour
Winton uses a dual-temperature strategy (93→88°C) chasing layers; Medina holds 91°C chasing consistency — one is a tuner, the other a metronome
Too weak
Grind half a step finer, or switch to 1:15
Can I use a V60
Yes — same conical geometry; flow differs slightly, so fine-tune the grind by total time
