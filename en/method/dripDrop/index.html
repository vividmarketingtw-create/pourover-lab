Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 180g)

Drip by drip → thin stream → medium stream
  1. 1. Drip stage (23g, about 1:30)
    At 0:00 · Pour 23g · up to 23g · 90s
    Rinse the filter and preheat the dripper, discard the water, then add and level the grounds.
    At 92°C, hold the spout close to the centre of the bed and let the water fall one drop at a time (not a thin stream — you should be able to see individual drops). Everything lands on that small centre patch and soaks outwards; over about 1:30 you put in 23g (1.5× the dose), and once coffee starts dripping into the server you can move on.
    There is no bloom here — the dripping stage is the bloom, in extreme slow motion.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 4.5-5 (like granulated sugar — coarser than a normal pour-over)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~28-30 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.8-3.0 turns
    ℹ️ Curated by PourOver Lab: the common Japanese drip-by-drip practice (the Kono / flannel lineage) — 15g / 180g (1:12), 92°C, a granulated-sugar grind, about 4 minutes in total, in the order drip → thin stream → medium stream. Two cues are used for the end of the drip stage: about a third of the bed wet, or the first drops of coffee falling into the server; in practice they arrive at much the same time.
    🌡 For dark roasts drop to 85-88°C — this technique was built for medium and darker roasts, and hot water drags the roast bitterness along with it.
    🫗 Gear: a Kono suits it best (no ribs up top, paper clinging to the wall, slow flow); a V60 or Origami works too, it just asks for more patience.
  2. 2. Thin stream to halfway (90g)
    At 1:30 · Pour 67g · up to 90g · 56s
    When the dripping is done, join the drops into a very thin stream, still in small circles at the centre: about 56s for 67g, up to 90g (half the total). The stream should be so fine that it barely disturbs the bed — this is the stage that builds the sweetness.
  3. 3. Medium stream to the total
    At 2:26 · Pour 90g · up to 180g · 45s
    Thicken the stream a little (still small circles), about 45s for the remaining 90g. Watch the water level, not the clock: stop when the bed rises close to the rim, resume when it falls back to half — this is the one rule that matters in drip-by-drip. Better to break it into several goes than to let water spill over the wall of grounds.
  4. 4. Drawdown (lift the dripper before the last drops)
    At After the pour · holding at 180g · 40s
    Let it drain; roughly 3:51 in total (the original 15g / 180g runs about 4 minutes).
    You can lift the dripper off a few drops before it runs dry — the tail end carries the harshest, most astringent liquid, and Japanese practice usually leaves it behind.
    💡 Bitter or slow → half a step coarser, or drop the water to 88-90°C; weak or sour → stretch the drip stage, or go half a step finer. This is a 1:12 concentrate: if it is too strong, just add hot water in the cup — the flavour holds up fine.

Full method guide

Origin

The common Japanese drip-by-drip practice, curated by PourOver Lab. It is one of the three classic Japanese pour-over techniques (the others being the three-stage method and the stirring method), and it is most closely tied to the Kono dripper and to flannel. The usual recipe is 15g / 180g (1:12), 92°C, a granulated-sugar grind and about four minutes in total, always in the order drip → thin stream → medium stream.

Core idea

The dripping stage replaces the bloom with individual drops of water — a tiny amount of water over a very long time, so the bed takes up water from the centre outwards and the carbon dioxide has all the time it needs to leave. A bed started that way is far less prone to channelling, so the thin and medium streams that follow can stack sweetness and body onto it steadily. It is the slowest and most patience-hungry recipe on this site, and what you get back is thick, sweet and round — the opposite end of the spectrum from the clean-and-bright school.

Key points

  • Prep: rinse the filter, preheat the dripper and server, level the bed
  • Drip stage: spout close to the centre of the bed, one drop at a time, about 1.5× the dose in water; move on when coffee starts falling into the server (the other common cue is about a third of the bed wet — much the same moment)
  • Thin stream: join the drops into a very fine stream, small circles at the centre, up to half the total water; the stream should barely disturb the bed
  • Medium stream: thicken it a little and finish the total. Watch the level, not the clock — stop when the bed rises close to the rim, resume when it drops back to half
  • Lift the dripper off a few drops before it runs dry — the tail is the harshest part, and Japanese practice usually leaves it behind
  • Gear: a Kono suits it best (no ribs up top, paper clinging to the wall, slow flow); a V60 or Origami works with more patience
  • 92°C is the baseline and dark roasts can drop to 85-88°C; this technique was built for medium and darker roasts

Beans it suits

Medium and medium-dark roasts are the natural fit — the slow drip stacks sweetness and body as high as they go. Dark roasts suit it too, just bring the temperature down

Less suited to

When you are in a hurry (four minutes and up, and you are standing over it for the first one or two); light roasts brewed for bright acidity

FAQ

How long do I keep dripping
Until the first coffee falls into the server, which is roughly when a third of the bed is wet. This site gives you a reference time for your dose, but the real cue is the drop you see
My hand aches — is there a shortcut
Hold the kettle low and rest your elbow on the counter. If you truly cannot keep it up, use an extremely fine stream instead; you lose some of the effect but not the brew
The bed has risen to the top
Stop, and start again when the level falls back to half. That is the only water-level rule in this technique — several small goes beat letting water spill over the wall of grounds
Bitter / slow
Grind half a step coarser, or drop the water to 88-90°C; you can also shorten the drip stage a little
Weak / sour
Stretch the drip stage (more drops, slower), or grind half a step finer
1:12 is too strong for me
Just add hot water in the cup — the flavour holds up fine. For a more systematic version, see the Bypass recipe on this site
