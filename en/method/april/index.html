Full method guide

Origin

April Coffee Roasters (Patrik Rolf's roastery in Copenhagen) publishes this as "How we brew coffee with a V60": 20g / 300g (1:15), 92°C, water at 90-110 ppm, a coarse grind, and six pours of 50g each starting 0:00 / 0:40 / 1:10 / 1:40 / 2:10 / 2:40, every one of them circling aggressively, 3:20-3:30 in total.

Core idea

The logic is grind coarse, pour often, agitate every time: a coarser grind lowers the intensity of each individual pour and cuts astringency, and six pours put the total extraction back on. The result is a cup that is clean and full at once. Six equal pours on a fixed clock is also far simpler to run than it looks — all you have to remember is one pour every 30 seconds, always the same grams.

Key points

  • Rinse the filter and preheat the dripper, discard the water, then add the grounds
  • Six equal pours (total water ÷ 6) starting 0:00 / 0:40 / 1:10 / 1:40 / 2:10 / 2:40 (gaps 40/30/30/30/30s — original values, locked on this site)
  • Circle assertively on every pour so the bed is agitated — the original word is aggressively
  • Grind coarse: about a step coarser than a normal pour-over (Feima 600N 5-5.5, Comandante 28-32 clicks)
  • The original specifies 90-110 ppm water; ordinary filtered water is fine at home, and hard water is worth softening
  • 3:20-3:30 in total; once the last pour is in, leave it alone
  • 20g is the original dose — this site scales everything proportionally to yours

Beans it suits

Light and light-medium coffees with floral-fruity character suit it best — the coarse grind plus many pours is unusually good at pulling out translucent florals and juiciness

Less suited to

Dark roasts (a coarse grind under-extracts them and the cup goes hollow); occasions when you want a heavy body

FAQ

Does the grind really have to be that coarse
Yes, it is the core of the recipe. Coarser grounds lower per-pour intensity and astringency, and the six pours put the total extraction back. Running it at a normal medium-fine grind tends to over-extract and turn bitter
How aggressive is "aggressively"
A step up from a normal pour-over: wider circles, a stream that is not too thin, enough that you can see the bed moving. Still keep off the filter wall
Weak / sour
Grind half a step finer, or raise the water to 93-94°C; also check that all six pours hit their grams — the later pours are where people get lazy
Bitter / slow
Go another half step coarser. Bitterness at a coarse grind usually means too many fines, which is a burr-quality problem
Can I use fewer pours
You can, but then it is a different recipe. Fewer pours needs a finer grind to compensate, or the cup goes thin
