Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

Bloom + One Continuous Pour
  1. 1. Bloom pour
    At 0:00 · Pour 30g · up to 30g · 30s
    Bloom with 92°C water - blooming releases CO2 from the grounds so the later pour contacts the coffee evenly. Pour in 2x the dose and wait about 20 seconds for the grounds to fully wet and degas.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 3.5 (texture like fine sugar, on the fine side)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~20-23 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.0-2.3 turns
  2. 2. Finish in one pour
    At 0:30 · Pour 195g · up to 225g · 56s
    92°C, pour steadily without interruption, spiraling from the center outward and back to the center - about 56 seconds (about 3.5 g/s)
  3. 3. Let it drain
    At After pour · holding at 225g · 30s
    No stirring, no swirling - wait until the coffee has fully drained, then remove the dripper

Full method guide

Origin

The most minimal pour-over style: strip away pulse pouring and complete the extraction with one steady pour. It has no single inventor. The mainstream definition is bloom + one continuous pour — two pours in total, which is the version used here; a second school skips the bloom entirely and pours straight through (usually with cooler water and a shorter total time). Both go by the same name, so check which one a recipe means.

Core idea

Reduce human variables and let the water pass evenly through the coffee bed on its own. The simpler the operation, the fewer the variables, the more consistent the cup.

Key points

  • Recommended 92°C, suited to stable extraction of medium roasts. The site converts automatically by roast level: light 93°C, dark 87°C — the step card shows the converted temperature
  • After roughly a 30-second bloom (the site scales it to 25-50s by dose), pour all the remaining water in one steady stream
  • Keep the stream thin and steady, spiralling from the center outward and back to the center, never breaking the stream (about 3.5 g/s)
  • No pauses, no stages, no stirring
  • Hold the same pour height and speed throughout

Beans it suits

Best with medium roasts thanks to the wide margin for error; dark roasts suit it just as well — their looser structure extracts fast, and a single undisturbed pour is less likely to over-extract into bitterness. Also a good baseline test for a new bean

Less suited to

Very light, dense roasts (one pour gives too little agitation and under-extracts into sourness); and scenarios needing fine sweet-acid control (Kasuya 4:6 does it better)

FAQ

Too weak / under-extracted
Grind half a step finer first — with no pulses to correct with, grind is the dominant variable here. If that is not enough, tighten the ratio to 1:14 or slow the pour to lengthen contact
Too bitter / drying
Grind half a step coarser, or drop the water 2°C. Do not try to fix bitterness by pouring faster — a faster pour raises both agitation and the water level, and in practice usually extracts more, not less
Total time much longer than target
Grind is too fine or there are too many fines (one continuous pour compacts the bed easily) — go half a step coarser. If it is always long, correct the wait with 'My flow'
Total time clearly too short
Grind is too coarse or the pour is too fast — go half a step finer and thin the stream
Flavor is one-note
Clean consistency is the point of Single Pour; try other methods for more layers
