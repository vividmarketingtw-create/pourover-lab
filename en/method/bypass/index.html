Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 240g)

1:12 concentrate + a final dilution
  1. 1. Bloom
    At 0:00 · Pour 45g · up to 45g · 40s
    Rinse the filter and preheat the dripper and server, discard the water, then add and level the grounds.
    Pour 45g at 94°C (3× the dose) and swirl the dripper gently so every ground is wet; wait until 40s.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 3-3.5 (half a step finer than usual — the whole point of bypass is that you can grind fine and let the dilution handle the bitterness)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~20-23 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.0-2.3 turns
    ℹ️ Curated by PourOver Lab: bypass means adding part of the recipe's water straight to the cup instead of through the bed. Here you brew 180g of concentrate at 1:12 and then add 60g of water to land at 1:16. It isolates the fruit acids while diluting bitterness and papery notes; the cost is a little less aroma.
    🏆 It got popular enough at the World AeroPress Championship to be banned outright — which tells you how well it works.
  2. 2. Pour 2 (to 60% of the brew water)
    At 0:40 · Pour 63g · up to 108g · 40s
    94°C, about 13s for 63g, circling to cover the bed, up to 108g. Wait until 1:20 for the last pour.
  3. 3. Pour 3 (to 180g of concentrate)
    At 1:20 · Pour 72g · up to 180g · 14s
    About 14s for 72g, finishing with small circles at the centre, up to 180g — that is all the water the bed gets, not the recipe total. A light swirl afterwards brings the grounds down off the wall.
  4. 4. Wait for drawdown
    At After the pour · holding at 180g · 40s
    Let the concentrate drain, about 0:40 more. What is in the server now is a very strong coffee (1:12) — smell it and take a small sip so you know what it tastes like.
  5. 5. Bypass: add 60g of water
    At After drawdown · Pour 60g · up to 240g · 15s
    Pour 60g of hot water straight into the server (not through the dripper) and stir once: the cup goes from 1:12 to 1:16.
    Add about half of it (30g) first and taste — beans differ in how much dilution they take.
    💡 Water at the same temperature is the standard move; room-temperature water works too and tastes the same, it just cools the cup faster. Too weak, add less; too bitter, add more — this is the one recipe you can still rescue in the server.

Full method guide

Origin

Bypass is not one person's recipe but a general technique: part of the recipe's water goes straight into the cup instead of through the bed. How popular did it get in specialty coffee? The World AeroPress Championship banned it outright because so many competitors were using it. The version on this site brews a 1:12 concentrate and then dilutes back to 1:16.

Core idea

The later the liquid comes through a brew, the higher the share of bitterness, astringency and papery notes it carries. Bypass simply stops extracting that far — you pull the best of the early fruit acidity and sweetness with less water, then make up the missing volume with plain water. The result is clear acidity and very little bitterness, at the cost of a little aroma. It is also the only recipe here you can rescue after the fact: if you ground too fine and over-extracted, more bypass water brings it back into drinkable range.

Key points

  • Two ratios: a 1:12 brew (75% of the total water) and a 1:16 final cup (after dilution)
  • You can grind half a step finer than usual — the point of bypass is that you can afford to, because the dilution handles the bitterness
  • Bloom at 3× the dose for a fixed 40s, with a gentle swirl so everything is wet
  • Pour 2 takes you to 60% of the brew water; 40s later pour 3 finishes the brew water, with a light swirl to bring the grounds off the wall
  • After drawdown, add the bypass water straight into the server (not through the dripper) and stir once
  • Add half of it first and taste — beans differ in how much dilution they take
  • Water at the same temperature is standard; room-temperature water tastes the same, it just cools the cup faster

Beans it suits

Almost anything. It is excellent for showing off fruit acidity in light roasts, and just as useful for taming bitterness in dark or inexpensive coffee. Big doses (30-40g) suit it especially well — less brew water means a bed that never gets too deep

Less suited to

When you want the fullest possible aroma (dilution always costs some); competition or any time you need to present a coffee exactly as it brews

FAQ

Does diluting make it watery
No — you are diluting a cup that was deliberately over-concentrated, so the final strength matches a normal brew. What thins out is aroma, not taste
How much water should I add
Add half, taste, then decide. The 1:12 → 1:16 here is a good starting point; in practice anything between 1:13 → 1:15 is common
Too bitter
Add more water (toward 1:17), or grind half a step coarser. Bitterness is the easiest problem to fix in a bypass brew
Too weak / thin
Add less water (toward 1:14), or tighten the brew ratio to 1:11
Can I dilute with cold water
Yes, and then it is half an iced brew. If you want it cold, use the Flash Brew recipe on this site instead — counting the ice as the dilution is more precise
