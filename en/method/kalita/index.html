Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 240g)

Bloom + three equal pours · one every 35s
  1. 1. Bloom
    At 0:00 · Pour 30g · up to 30g · 30s
    Rinse the filter gently: the pleats of a wave filter are its flow structure — wet it with a little water from the centre and preheat the dripper, but do not flatten the pleats with a big stream. Discard the water, add the grounds and tap the dripper to level the bed (a flat bed is the whole advantage here).
    Pour twice the dose (30g) at 92°C in small circles from the centre so all the grounds are wet, and wait until 30s before the first pour; about 22s of degassing after the pour. No swirl needed — a flat bed does not need your help to level.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 4-4.5 (half a step coarser than a V60 medium-fine — three small holes already flow slowly, and too fine drags)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~24-27 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.4-2.7 turns
    ℹ️ This is the common flat-bed practice, curated by PourOver Lab: 1:16, 92-93°C, 2× bloom for 30s, then three equal pours every 30-40s, about 2:45-3:15 total — the Kalita guides from Kaldi's, Ozone and others all sit in this range. The flat bed and three holes keep water in the bed longer and level it for you, which makes this the most forgiving percolation method — and a great first recipe to practise on.
  2. 2. Pour 1: 70g (to 100g)
    At 0:30 · Pour 70g · up to 100g · 35s
    Start at 0:30, pour 70g in about 18s (about 3.9 g/s). Coin-sized circles, spout low, slowly out from the centre and back; never hit the filter wall, and keep the water only just above the bed — on a flat-bed dripper a high water level bypasses along the edge. Wait until 1:05 for pour 2.
  3. 3. Pour 2: 70g (to 170g)
    At 1:05 · Pour 70g · up to 170g · 35s
    Start at 1:05, same small circles and speed, 70g in about 18s. Read the bed colour: an even dark surface means the water is spread evenly; a paler patch is where you missed. Wait until 1:40 for the last pour.
  4. 4. Pour 3: 70g (to 240g)
    At 1:40 · Pour 70g · up to 240g · 18s
    Last pour, 70g in about 18s, to 240g total. Then leave it alone — no stir, no swirl; let the three holes drain in their own time.
  5. 5. Wait for drawdown
    At After the pour · holding at 240g · 60s
    Three holes drain slowly; expect about 2:58 in total. The finished bed should be flat, with no obvious high or low spots — that is your proof of even pouring.
    💡 Well past 3:30 or bitter → grind half a step coarser; under 2:30 or sour → half a step finer. On-screen times use a V60 as the baseline; pick Kalita Wave on the Brew Tools page and the timeline slows down by about 10%.

Full method guide

Origin

The common practice for flat-bed drippers (Kalita Wave 185 / 155), curated by PourOver Lab: 1:16, 92-93°C, a 2× bloom for 30s, then three equal pours every 30-40s, about 2:45-3:15 total. The Kalita guides from Kaldi's Coffee, Ozone Coffee and others all sit in this range; there is no single inventor.

Core idea

A flat bed, three small holes and a wave filter keep water in the bed longer and level it for you — the most forgiving percolation method there is. It hands the two hardest things to learn ("keep the bed flat, spread the water evenly") to the hardware, so all you practise is making three pours the same. The cup is round, full-bodied and not very acidic — the everyday recipe in many cafés.

Key points

  • Rinse gently: the pleats of the wave filter are its flow structure — wet it with a little water from the centre; do not flatten them
  • After adding the grounds, tap the dripper to level the bed; 2× bloom, fixed 30s, no swirl
  • Then three equal pours, one every 35s (common practice 30-40s), about 15s each
  • Coin-sized circles, spout low, never hit the filter wall; keep the water only just above the bed — on a flat-bed dripper a high water level bypasses along the edge
  • No stir and no swirl after the pours; let the three holes drain. The finished bed should be flat
  • Grind half a step coarser than a V60 medium-fine (medium) — three holes already flow slowly, and too fine pushes you past 4 minutes
  • On-screen times use a V60 baseline; pick Kalita Wave on the Brew Tools page and the timeline slows by about 10%

Beans it suits

Medium and medium-dark roasts fit best — the slow flat-bed flow brings out roundness and sweetness; naturals and honeys are especially pleasing here. Light roasts work too, a little softer than on a V60

Less suited to

When you want maximum brightness and tea-like clarity (the faster V60 recipes — Hoffmann or Wölfl — do that better)

FAQ

I do not own a Kalita Wave
An Origami with a wave filter is nearly identical; a V60 can follow the same three-pour structure, it just flows faster and tastes brighter — grind half a step finer to compensate
Weak / sour
Grind half a step finer, or raise the water to 93-94°C; check that all three pours reached their full grams
Bitter / drains too slowly
Grind half a step coarser — the three holes are slow by design and too fine is the usual cause. Also check whether the filter pleats got flattened during rinsing (a collapsed filter drains even slower)
The bed is not flat after drawdown
The water was not spread evenly: make the circles smaller and the speed steadier, and start every pour from the centre. That is exactly what this recipe is for
Can I use two or four pours instead
Yes — with the same total water, more pours mean a steadier level and less agitation, so a softer cup; three is simply the easiest to remember and the most common
