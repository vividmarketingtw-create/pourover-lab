Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 251g)

Bloom + Two Main Pours
  1. 1. Bloom pour
    At 0:00 · Pour 30g · up to 30g · 45s
    Rinse the paper filter with hot water and preheat the dripper, discard the water, add the grounds and press a small well into the center of the dry bed (both are part of the original technique), then start the timer.
    Use freshly boiled water (about 100°C - Hoffmann stresses the hotter the better). Pour in 2x the dose, then gently swirl the dripper to wet all the grounds — swirl, do not stir. If the bed puffs up (like a burger), the beans are fresh! The bloom is fixed at 45 seconds (the original value; it does not scale with dose), leaving about 35 seconds of degassing after the pour.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 3.5-4 (texture like fine sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~21-24 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.1-2.4 turns
  2. 2. First main pour (up to 60% of total)
    At 0:45 · Pour 121g · up to 151g · 30s
    Keep the water near boiling (~100°C) and pour steadily in circles from the center, finishing in about 30 seconds (about 4 g/s).
    = the original 30-second window (0:45 → 1:15).
  3. 3. Second main pour (up to 100%)
    At 1:15 · Pour 100g · up to 251g · 30s
    ~100°C, same technique but a little slower than the first pour, finishing in about 30 seconds (about 3.3 g/s).
    = the original 30-second window (1:15 → 1:45).
  4. 4. Stir and swirl
    At 1:45 · holding at 251g · 15s
    First stir once clockwise and once counterclockwise with a stirrer to knock grounds off the paper walls; then, once the water level has dropped a little, swirl the dripper gently to level the bed. The order matters — the original technique does both; skip the swirl and the bed sits uneven
  5. 5. Let it drain
    At After pour · holding at 251g · 15s
    Wait for the coffee to drain fully - about 2:15 overall - then remove the dripper and enjoy.
    💡 Hoffmann's own benchmark is 30g / 500g finishing at about 3:30. If your total is far off, correct the wait with "My flow" first, then consider changing the grind.

Full method guide

Origin

Proposed by British coffee expert James Hoffmann (YouTube, The Ultimate V60 Technique), known for simple steps and highly even extraction. Hoffmann stresses using water as hot as possible (freshly boiled), arguing high heat helps a more complete extraction.

Core idea

A thorough bloom, two-stage pouring, and a final stir and swirl produce even extraction of the grounds and a clean, bright cup. The hotter the water the better (near boiling); dark roasts can go somewhat cooler.

Key points

  • Original reference: 30g / 500g (1:16.7), main pour 1 at 8 g/s and main pour 2 at 6.7 g/s, drained by about 3:30
  • Setup: rinse the paper filter and preheat the dripper, discard the water, add the grounds and press a small well into the center of the dry bed before starting the timer
  • Water: freshly boiled (near 100°C) - the core signature of this method
  • Bloom: pour 2x the dose, gently swirl the dripper to wet all the grounds, wait 45 seconds - this 45s is fixed and does not scale with dose
  • First main pour: start at 45 seconds, pour to 60% of total water within about 30 seconds
  • Second main pour: pour to 100% of total water within about 30 seconds
  • Stir: immediately after the last pour, stir once clockwise and once counterclockwise (stir first, swirl after)
  • Swirl: gently swirl the dripper to level the bed, then wait for full drawdown

Beans it suits

Washed light roasts show off the clean, bright profile best; anaerobic beans also suit this simple workflow, which reveals their complexity

Less suited to

Dark roasts (very hot water over-extracts them - drop to 90-93°C if needed)

FAQ

Too sour
Grind half a step finer or extend the total brew time
Too bitter
Stir more gently; for dark roasts drop the water to 90-93°C
Too weak
Change the ratio from 1:16.7 to 1:15
Drawdown takes too long
Grind is too fine - go coarser
