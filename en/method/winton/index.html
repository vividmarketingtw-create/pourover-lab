Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

Five Unequal Pours · Two Temperatures · No Stirring
  1. 1. Pour 1: 38g (93°C) · bloom
    At 0:00 · Pour 38g · up to 38g · 35s
    Pour the first 38g at 93°C (about 8s). The pour path is what replaces stirring here: pour into the centre to flood the bed, then circle outward to agitate the rest, then come back to the centre for control.
    Never use a stirrer and never swirl the dripper — Winton explicitly does not stir; all the agitation comes from the pour itself. The bloom is fixed at 35 seconds (he tested 30 and 45 before settling on it), then a pour every 30 seconds.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 4.5-5 (texture like coarse sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~28-30 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.8-3.0 turns (he uses Comandante ~30 clicks / Kinu 3.6)
    ℹ️ Competition original: 20g / 300g on a metal cone V60 (he chose metal for clarity and brightness versus a flat bed — metal actually holds heat less well), five unequal pours of 50/70/60/60/60g at 0:00 / 0:35 / 1:05 / 1:35 / 2:05, about 2:40 total.
  2. 2. Pour 2: 53g (93°C)
    At 0:35 · Pour 53g · up to 91g · 30s
    Still 93°C, pour 2 of 53g (about 12s, roughly 4.4 g/s). Centre, then circles, then back to centre — decisive, no hesitation.
  3. 3. Pour 3: 45g (93°C)
    At 1:05 · Pour 45g · up to 136g · 30s
    Still 93°C, pour 3 of 45g (about 10s, roughly 4.5 g/s). Centre, then circles, then back to centre — decisive, no hesitation.
  4. 4. Pour 4: 45g (88°C) · switch to cool
    At 1:35 · Pour 45g · up to 181g · 30s
    Switch to the cooler kettle: 88°C. Pour 4 of 45g (about 10s), same centre-circle-centre path. The drop mimics a kettle cooling naturally and adds sweetness late without sharpening the acidity.
  5. 5. Pour 5: 44g (88°C)
    At 2:05 · Pour 44g · up to 225g · 10s
    Still 88°C, final pour of 44g (about 10s). Once it is in, do not touch it — no stirring, no swirling, just let it drain.
  6. 6. Let it drain
    At 2:15 · holding at 225g · 15s
    Wait for the coffee to drain fully, about 2:30 overall.
    💡 The original 20g / 300g drains at about 2:40. Use his own rule to dial in: too slow or bitter, grind coarser; too fast or sour, grind finer.

Full method guide

Origin

Created by Matt Winton, the 2021 World Brewers Cup champion (Winton is Australian and competed for Switzerland). This site uses the competition recipe: 20g / 300g (1:15), a metal cone V60, a fairly coarse grind (he uses Comandante ~30 clicks / Kinu 3.6), two kettles, and five unequal pours of 50/70/60/60/60g at 0:00 / 0:35 / 1:05 / 1:35 / 2:05, about 2:40 total. (He later published a five-equal-pour home version too; this site uses the competition one.)

Core idea

Two ideas carry this method: two temperatures and no stirring. The first three pours at 93°C pull the flavour out; pours four and five switch to an 88°C kettle, deliberately mimicking the curve of a kettle cooling on its own, adding sweetness late without sharpening acidity. And he never stirs manually (the Barista Hustle interview is literally titled 'No Stir') — every bit of agitation comes from the pour path: flood the bed from the centre, circle outward to agitate the rest, then return to the centre for control.

Key points

  • Gear: a metal cone V60 — he chose metal for clarity and brightness versus a flat bed, not for heat retention; metal actually holds heat less well than ceramic
  • Fairly coarse grind: he uses Comandante ~30 clicks / Kinu 3.6 (about Feima 600N 4.5-5), coarser than a typical V60
  • Five unequal pours: 50 / 70 / 60 / 60 / 60g (the second is the biggest)
  • The bloom is fixed at 35 seconds (he tested 30 and 45 first), then a pour every 30 seconds
  • Pours 1-3 at 93°C, pours 4-5 at 88°C — two kettles, or let one cool naturally
  • No stirring and no swirling at any point; agitation comes from the centre-flood, circle-agitate, return-to-centre pour path
  • The original 20g / 300g drains at about 2:40

Beans it suits

Ethiopian naturals (florals and fruit come through fully); East African light roasts such as Kenya and Rwanda also suit it well

Less suited to

Medium-dark roasts and darker (the two-temperature idea does little for them, and the hot opening pulls bitterness first)

FAQ

What if I only have one kettle
That works — after the third pour, take the lid off and let it sit 1-2 minutes to fall to about 88°C. The two-kettle setup is only mimicking that natural cooling curve anyway
Too bitter / draining too slowly
Grind half a step coarser — his own rule. Then check the late pours really did drop to 88°C
Too sour / draining too fast
Grind half a step finer — again his rule. You can also raise the cool kettle from 88°C to 90°C
Can I stir or swirl
No. Winton explicitly does not stir, and that is central to the method — all agitation is delegated to the pour path (centre, circles, back to centre). Add stirring and it stops being this recipe
What exactly is the pour path
Start in the dead centre to flood the bed, circle outward to bring the edges into play, then come back to the centre to steady the stream. Every pour completes all three
