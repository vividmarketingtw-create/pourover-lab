Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

Double bloom + one pour
  1. 1. First bloom
    At 0:00 · Pour 45g · up to 45g · 30s
    Rinse the filter with hot water first (preheats everything; discard), add the coffee and level the bed.
    Pour 45g at 93°C (3× the dose). Anywhere from 5-10 g/s is fine, just don't blast it. No swirl — Hedrick points out that swirling slows the flow, especially with slow filters; the second bloom will saturate everything anyway. Wait until 0:30.
    Grind reference: start at Feima 600N 3.5-4 (like caster sugar)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~21-24 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.1-2.4 turns
    ℹ️ Original (Lance Hedrick, 2026-04-27, "The Last V60 Recipe You'll Ever Need"): 15g / 225g (1:15), 93°C; bloom 45g at 0:00 (3×), second bloom 45g at 0:30 (90g total), then pour straight to 225g at 1:00 (9-10 g/s, about 12-13 s), total time 2:00-2:30. He deliberately gives no grind size: every grinder and every filter drains differently, so "grind until the total time lands in 2:00-2:30" is the whole rule.
    🌡 Water by roast: ultra-light 93-96°C (he stays at 93), American light to light-medium 90-93°C, anything darker below 90°C.
  2. 2. Second bloom
    At 0:30 · Pour 45g · up to 90g · 30s
    Pour another 45g at 93°C (again 3× the dose), up to 90g total. This pushes out the CO₂ the first bloom left trapped — you will see the bed bubble up again. The point is that the main pour then doesn't get bubbles rising through it and water routing around them (channeling). Still no swirl. Wait until 1:00.
  3. 3. One pour to the total
    At 1:00 · Pour 135g · up to 225g · 14s
    93°C, fast: 9-10 g/s, about 14 s to pour the remaining 135g up to 225g. Keep the kettle over the centre in coin-sized circles and deliberately shovel the bed around — this recipe extracts through the agitation of this one pour, so don't be gentle.
  4. 4. Wait for drawdown
    At After the pour · holding at 225g · 61s
    Let it drain, about 1:01 more; overall target about 2:15.
    💡 The only thing you tune is total time (the original 15g target is 2:00-2:30; for larger doses this site stretches it with its drawdown model): faster than target → grind finer; slower → grind coarser. Emergency fix when it's too coarse and draining too fast: a gentle swirl during drawdown adds 10-15 s. Too strong: go coarser + swirl, or open the ratio to 1:16-1:17; want it stronger and more acidic: 1:13.

Full method guide

Origin

Lance Hedrick's "The Last V60 Recipe You'll Ever Need", published in April 2026 (he calls it the "one and done"). He is one of the most influential coffee educators on YouTube and has coached several World Barista Championship competitors. He says he quietly worked on this recipe for close to two years and had well over a hundred people test it with different grinders and filters, the goal being "whatever grinder and filter you have, follow this and get a good cup". The recipe itself is minimal: 15g / 225g, 93°C, bloom 45g at 0:00, bloom another 45g at 0:30, pour straight to the total at 1:00, total time 2:00-2:30.

Core idea

One idea: "don't dial the grind by how it looks, dial it by total time". He argues the biggest problem with online recipes is instructions like "grind like coarse salt" — your grinder and his produce different fines and particle shapes, and filter paper alone can shift the brew by 30-40 seconds. So he made the structure as simple as it gets (two blooms, one pour) and collapsed every variable into a single check: if the total time lands in 2:00-2:30, you're there. The double bloom clears CO₂ before the main pour to avoid channeling; the main pour is deliberately fast and aggressive (9-10 g/s) so agitation drives extraction to about 18.5-19% and a TDS around 1.3 — the concentration he found most people prefer.

Key points

  • Rinse the filter with hot water and discard; level the bed
  • 0:00 first bloom: 3× the dose (15g → 45g), 5-10 g/s, no swirl (swirling slows the flow, most noticeably with slow filters)
  • 0:30 second bloom: another 3× (90g total) to push out trapped CO₂; still no swirl
  • 1:00 pour straight to the total (225g): 9-10 g/s, kettle over the centre in coin-sized circles, deliberately disturbing the bed; about 12-13 s in the original
  • Then let it drain; total time target 2:00-2:30 — 2:00 is his minimum for full flavour expression
  • Adjust the grind only by total time: too fast → finer, too slow → coarser. Emergency fix for a too-coarse grind: a gentle swirl during drawdown adds 10-15 s
  • Water by roast: ultra-light 93-96°C (he almost always uses 93), American light to light-medium 90-93°C, anything darker below 90°C
  • 1:15 is the starting ratio; play between 1:13 (stronger, more acidity) and 1:17 (lighter) without touching the structure or the pouring
  • He says it also works in other pour-over brewers, not just the V60

Beans it suits

Almost everything — that is the design goal. Ultra-light at 93-96°C, medium around 90°C, dark lower still

Less suited to

If you want a very light, tea-like extraction (he himself usually drinks a lighter two-pour); if you want to practise staged pouring technique (this recipe deliberately removes it)

FAQ

Why no grind setting
Deliberate. Fines ratio and particle shape vary hugely between grinders, and filters can add 30-40 s, so the same "setting" is not the same thing in your hands. The 3.5-4 here is only a starting point — brew, look at the total time, adjust
Does the second bloom really matter
His reasoning: with a gassy coffee the first bloom can't release it all, so during the main pour CO₂ rises through the bed and water routes around it (channeling). On the second bloom you will see the bed bubble again — that is the gas being pushed out
How fast is the main pour
9-10 g/s — fast and aggressive, kettle over the centre in small circles. This recipe extracts by stirring the bed up with this one pour, so don't be gentle
It finishes before 2:00
Grind finer. Quick fix in the moment: a gentle swirl during drawdown adds 10-15 s
Too strong
Grind coarser and add a swirl to keep the time, or open the ratio to 1:16 or 1:17; don't change the pour structure
Which water temperature
Ultra-light 93-96°C (he uses 93 almost always); American light to light-medium 90-93; anything darker below 90
