Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

Ten small pours
  1. 1. Pour 1 (bloom)
    At 0:00 · Pour 23g · up to 23g · 30s
    🔻 Gear: the original specifies the HARIO V60 NEO — its 72 ultra-fine upper ribs distribute each small pour evenly down the wall, which is exactly what ten little pours need, while nine converging ribs at the base speed the drawdown. A standard V60 brews it perfectly well (that is Kasuya's own everyday dripper) — it just runs slightly longer.
    Pour the first 23g at 95-96°C (~6s) — this doubles as the bloom. Super-coarse grounds degas fast with almost no bitterness; wait until 30s before pour 2.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 7-8 (near the coarsest end of the dial; flat burrs lose uniformity here, so some fines are expected)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~40-45 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~4.0-4.5 turns (extra-coarse, near French press)
    ℹ️ Kasuya's 2026 recipe — he calls it the Multi-Pour Method; "Neo Brew" and "Click 45" are nicknames, not his names. The original is 20g / 300g: pour 1 of 30g, wait until 0:30, then a 30g pour every 15 seconds for 10 pours (the last at 2:30), about 3:30 overall. This site now locks the 15s cadence and the 30s bloom to those original values — they no longer move with your flow setting; only the drawdown wait does.
  2. 2. Pour 2
    At 0:30 · Pour 23g · up to 46g · 15s
    95-96°C, one pour every 15s, 23g each. Repeated small pours re-wet the bed, stacking sweetness and body ("additive" extraction)
  3. 3. Pour 3
    At 0:45 · Pour 23g · up to 69g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  4. 4. Pour 4
    At 1:00 · Pour 23g · up to 92g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  5. 5. Pour 5
    At 1:15 · Pour 23g · up to 115g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  6. 6. Pour 6
    At 1:30 · Pour 23g · up to 138g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  7. 7. Pour 7
    At 1:45 · Pour 23g · up to 161g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  8. 8. Pour 8
    At 2:00 · Pour 23g · up to 184g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  9. 9. Pour 9
    At 2:15 · Pour 23g · up to 207g · 15s
    95-96°C, steady small pour of 23g (~6s, about 3.8 g/s)
  10. 10. Pour 10 (final)
    At 2:30 · Pour 18g · up to 225g · 6s
    Final pour. Then let it draw down — the coarse bed drains fast; total time ~2:44
  11. 11. Wait for drawdown
    At After pours · holding at 225g · 8s
    Let it drain fully. If total time runs long, grind even coarser; brewed right, this cup is thick and sweet with almost no bitterness

Full method guide

Origin

Tetsu Kasuya's ten-pour recipe, released in 2026 (widely nicknamed "Neo Brew", though Kasuya does not call it that) — exactly ten years after his 2016 World Brewers Cup title. It marks a shift from the 4:6's "subtractive" philosophy (avoiding negatives) to an "additive" one: actively stacking sweetness and body.

Core idea

The core is the combination of super-coarse grind × high heat × many small pours: near-French-press coarseness extracts almost no bitterness, which frees you to use 95-96°C water and ten pours to push extraction up; a pour every 15 seconds keeps re-wetting the bed, building a syrupy, sweet cup.

Key points

  • Gear: the original specifies the HARIO V60 NEO (the January 2026 V60 redesign — 72 ultra-fine upper ribs plus 9 converging ribs at the base, built so even small pours distribute evenly); Kasuya says a standard V60 is completely fine, it just runs a little longer
  • Grind close to French press coarseness — the whole recipe depends on it
  • Water at 95-96°C, hotter than the 4:6's 92-93°C
  • A fixed 30s bloom, then a pour every 15 seconds, 1/10 of the water each time — both numbers are the original values and are locked here, unaffected by gear or flow settings
  • Small steady pours; no fancy technique needed
  • Kasuya's original (20g / 300g): pours at 0:00 / 0:30 / 0:45 / 1:00 ... / 2:30, ten in all, about 3:30 total. The pour cadence here matches the original second for second (0:00-2:30 identical); only the final drawdown differs — a super-coarse bed needs just 10-20s in the model, so totals land around 2:50-3:00 rather than 3:30. Landing near 3:30 in real life is normal (the original grinder and dripper drain more slowly); but well past 4 minutes means the grind is still not coarse enough

Beans it suits

Light to medium roasts; great for beans where you want sweetness and body — and for anyone who finds pour-over "too sour and thin"

Less suited to

When you want bright acidity and a light body (Hoffmann or the Wölfl fast-flow suit that better)

FAQ

My grinder can't go that coarse
Set it to ~80% of its coarsest setting and start 1°C lower, then adjust by taste
Why doesn't the hot water make it bitter
Bitterness mostly comes from fines and over-extracted surface area; the super-coarse grind removes both, so the heat just completes the sweetness
Ten-Pour or 4:6
Want to tune acid/sweet balance and layers → 4:6; want thick sweetness and easy repeatability → Ten-Pour
Too weak
Grind 2-3 steps finer, or stretch the interval to 20 seconds
