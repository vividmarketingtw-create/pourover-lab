Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 225g)

Three pours · 6 / 4 / 5 g/s
  1. 1. First pour (bloom) · 6 g/s
    At 0:00 · Pour 56g · up to 56g · 18s
    Origami with a wave filter (Kalita 155 or 185; the M dripper takes a 185). Rinse the filter with hot water, preheat, discard. Add the coffee and level the bed.
    At 94°C pour 56g (25% of the total) fast, about 6 g/s, roughly 9 s, so the water covers the whole bed in one go. Then wait until 0:18.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 4-4.5 (like granulated sugar, a medium grind; the original was about 780 μm)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~24-27 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.4-2.7 turns
    ℹ️ Original (Du Jianing, 2019 WBrC champion, Boston): 16g / 240g (1:15), 94°C, Origami + wave filter; 60g at 0:00 (6 g/s), 80g at 0:18 (4 g/s), 100g to 240g at 0:56 (5 g/s), drained by about 1:40-1:50, roughly 190ml in the cup. On stage she also ground in two passes (coarse first, blew off the chaff, then reground to target) for a tighter particle spread — not required at home, but it explains how this brew is both fast and clean.
  2. 2. Second pour · 4 g/s
    At 0:18 · Pour 75g · up to 131g · 38s
    94°C, slow down to about 4 g/s, roughly 19 s for 75g, up to 131g. This is the slowest of the three — she deliberately alternated between 4, 5 and 6 g/s; the slow pour keeps the water level steady and the bed undisturbed. Then wait until 0:56.
  3. 3. Third pour · 5 g/s
    At 0:56 · Pour 94g · up to 225g · 19s
    94°C, about 5 g/s, roughly 19 s for the remaining 94g, up to 225g. Circle the kettle over the whole bed, then leave it alone.
  4. 4. Wait for drawdown
    At After the pour · holding at 225g · 35s
    Let it drain, about 0:35 more; overall target about 1:50 (the 16g original finishes around 1:40-1:50).
    💡 The key here is speed: the wave filter keeps the flow steady and the Origami ribs keep it from choking, so the total time is much shorter than a typical V60 recipe. Draining too slowly → grind coarser; too fast and thin → grind finer. The bed should be flat with no high walls — that is what the wave filter buys you.

Full method guide

Origin

The recipe Du Jianing used to win the 2019 World Brewers Cup in Boston — China's first WBrC title. Her reason for choosing the Origami was simple: "water can go through the coffee quickly and achieve clarity of flavor". After that competition the Origami went from a niche Japanese dripper to a brewer the whole world recognised. The recipe: 16g / 240g (1:15), 94°C, Origami with a wave filter, three pours of 60, 80 and 100g at 0:00, 0:18 and 0:56 poured at 6, 4 and 5 g/s, drained by about 1:40-1:50.

Core idea

Two ideas: pour speed is her variable, not time — three pours at deliberately different speeds (fast, slow, medium): the fast pour covers the bed, the slow one steadies it, the medium one finishes; and trading equipment for clarity — an Origami with a wave filter, where the filter's pleats hold it off the wall and only the base touches, gives a flow that is steady without choking, so the whole brew is done in under two minutes with acidity and florals intact. On stage she also ground in two passes (coarse, chaff removed, then fine) for a tighter particle spread — the other half of why it is both fast and clean.

Key points

  • Origami with a Kalita wave filter (155 or 185; the M dripper takes a 185); rinse, preheat, discard
  • 0:00 first pour 60g (25% of the total) at about 6 g/s, covering the whole bed in one go
  • 0:18 second pour 80g (33%), slowed to about 4 g/s to hold the water level steady
  • 0:56 third pour 100g to 240g at about 5 g/s, circling over the whole bed
  • Drained by about 1:40-1:50, roughly 190ml in the cup
  • 94°C, medium grind (about 780 μm)
  • The original used a two-pass grind: coarse first, blow off the chaff, then regrind to target — skip it at home
  • A total time far shorter than a typical V60 recipe is normal here; don't grind finer just to "stretch it to three minutes"

Beans it suits

Light and light-medium floral coffees (she used a Ninety Plus Geisha); natural or anaerobic fruit-forward lots also suit it

Less suited to

Dark roasts (fast, hot extraction leaves mostly roast bitterness); anyone after a heavy body

FAQ

Do I need an Origami
This recipe was built for the Origami with a wave filter. In a Kalita Wave the three small holes slow it down a lot and the timing drifts; in a V60 with a cone filter it runs even faster — workable, but the bed is never as flat as with a wave filter
How do I practise the pour speeds
Use the scale: 6 g/s is about 10 s for 60g, 4 g/s is 20 s for 80g. A narrow-spout kettle helps. The timer shows the seconds for each pour — match the seconds and you are at the right speed
It drains in under two minutes — is that too weak
No, that is the intended rhythm. 1:15 with hot, fast pours is plenty; if it tastes thin, grind half a step finer rather than stretching the time
Do I need the two-pass grind
No. That is a competition-level detail. One pass to a medium grind is fine at home
Too sour / sharp
Grind half a step finer, or keep 94°C but slow the second pour a touch more
