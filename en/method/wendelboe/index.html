Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 231g)

Stirred bloom + continuous pour
  1. 1. Bloom and stir
    At 0:00 · Pour 28g · up to 28g · 30s
    Rinse the filter with hot water first (it preheats the dripper and server too — discard the water), add the grounds and level them.
    Pour 28g at 94°C (about 1.85× the dose), then stir with a spoon until every last bit of dry coffee is wet — Wendelboe's word is stir, not swirl; the point is that no dry pocket survives. Wait until 30s before the next pour.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 3.5-4 (like caster sugar — a normal filter grind)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~21-24 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.1-2.4 turns
    ℹ️ The original (Tim Wendelboe's official brewing guide): 65g of coffee per litre of water (his example is 32.5g / 500g, about 1:15.4), rinse the filter, bloom with about 60g and stir, at 0:30 pour up to 40% of the total water (200g of 500g), then keep pouring in circles until you reach the total, finishing by about 2:00-2:30, with roughly another minute of drawdown (3:00-3:30 in total). Stir the brew in the server before serving. Adjust the grind by total time: too fast, grind finer; too slow, grind coarser.
    ⚠️ The guide does not specify a water temperature; this site uses 94°C (Nordic light roasts are usually brewed with water just off the boil).
  2. 2. Pour to 40% of the total (92g)
    At 0:30 · Pour 64g · up to 92g · 32s
    94°C, about 32s for 64g, circling to cover the whole bed, up to 92g (40% of the total). Do not stop here — run straight on into the next pour; from 0:30 onwards this recipe is one unbroken stream.
  3. 3. Keep pouring to the total
    At 1:02 · Pour 139g · up to 231g · 68s
    Now pour continuously in circles, about 68s for the remaining 139g, up to 231g. Keep the water level as steady as you can — do not let the bed surface break through and get flooded again. The original finishes pouring at about 2:00-2:30; this site scales that to your dose.
  4. 4. Wait for drawdown
    At After the pour · holding at 231g · 25s
    Let it drain, about 0:25 more; roughly 2:45 in total.
    💡 Wendelboe judges by total time alone: much faster than target → grind finer; much slower → grind coarser. He does not change the pouring to fix the taste.
  5. 5. Stir the server, then pour out
    At After drawdown · holding at 231g · 10s
    The brew sits in layers in the server (what came through first is stronger). Give it a stir before you pour so two cups taste the same — it is the last step in the original, and the one most people skip.

Full method guide

Origin

The recipe in Tim Wendelboe's official brewing guide. He won the World Barista Championship in 2004 and went on to open his own roastery in Oslo, and he is one of the names most associated with the Nordic light-roast style. The recipe itself is simple: 65g of coffee per litre of water (his example is 32.5g / 500g, about 1:15.4), stir the bloom, at 0:30 pour up to 40% of the total water, then keep pouring continuously to the end — done pouring by about 2:00-2:30, with roughly another minute of drawdown.

Core idea

The point of this method is not the stages, it is never leaving a pocket of dry coffee: the bloom is stirred with a spoon (not swirled) so everything is wet, and after that a single unbroken stream carries the extraction home. All the adjustment lives in the grind — too fast, grind finer; too slow, grind coarser; the pouring never changes. That is the typical Nordic-roastery approach: the beans are roasted light, so the brew method should be simple, repeatable and free of extra variables.

Key points

  • Rinse the filter with hot water first, which preheats the dripper and server as well
  • Bloom with about 1.85× the dose and stir with a spoon until every ground is wet — the original word is stir, not swirl
  • At 0:30, pour up to 40% of the total water (200g of 500g in the original); that timing is an original value
  • Then keep pouring in circles until you reach the total, holding the water level steady so the bed never breaks through and gets flooded again
  • The original finishes pouring at about 2:00-2:30 and drains in roughly another minute, 3:00-3:30 in total
  • Stir the brew in the server before pouring out — the first liquid through is stronger, and without a stir two cups will not match
  • Adjust the grind by total time: much faster than target, grind finer; much slower, grind coarser
  • The guide does not specify a water temperature; this site uses 94°C (Nordic light roasts are usually brewed just off the boil)

Beans it suits

Nordic-style light and light-medium washed coffees are the natural fit — bright acidity and clarity are exactly what this recipe is after; light naturals work beautifully too

Less suited to

Dark roasts (a continuous pour extracts hard, and dark roasts turn ashy); occasions when you want a heavy body

FAQ

Do I really have to stir with a spoon
That is what the original says. A swirl will wet the bed too, but stirring is the surest way to get every ground wet, especially with light roasts that take up water slowly. Use a slim spoon, two or three turns, and do not fling grounds up onto the paper
My continuous pour is not steady
Practise the water level first: always keep a layer of water above the bed — pour faster as it runs low, ease off when it climbs. If it really falls apart, split it into two or three pours; that beats losing control
Too sour / too fast
Grind half a step finer. That is his own rule — judge by total time, do not start changing the pour
Too bitter / too slow
Grind half a step coarser; also check you are not stirring so hard that fines end up plastered on the filter
What water temperature
The guide does not say. This site defaults to 94°C; light roasts can go close to boiling, medium roasts are happy at 92-93°C
