Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee（15g / 225g）

Prep - Ice in the Server
  1. Add ice (40% of total water)
    At Prep · Pour 90g
    Put the ice in the server — it counts toward the total liquid, it is not an extra.
    ⚠️ Weigh the ice; never eyeball it — it is part of the recipe, and guessing throws the whole strength off.
    ⚠️ Ice goes in the server only — never on the bed or in the dripper. Chilling the bed causes severe under-extraction and is the most common way this method fails.
    The hot water is only 60% of the total, so the brew concentration is about 1.67x a normal pour-over (keeping flavor intact after dilution)
    ❄️ Small brews use little ice (90g) that melts fast - be sure to use large solid cubes, not crushed ice, to avoid over-dilution
Brew - Hot Concentrated Extraction
  1. 1. Bloom pour
    At 0:00 · Pour 30g · 30s
    Flash brew blooms hot (96°C) to maximize extraction from a smaller water volume — pour in 2x the dose (30g) and hold a fixed 30-second bloom (common practice is 30-45s), leaving about 22 seconds of degassing after the pour.
    Grind reference: Feima 600N setting 3-3.5 (texture like table salt, on the fine side)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~18-20 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~1.8-2.0 turns
  2. 2. Main pour (only the remaining 60% hot water)
    At 0:30 · Pour 105g · 35s
    96°C, pour steadily until the hot water is used up, about 35 seconds (about 3 g/s). Note: brew with the hot water only - the ice waits in the server
  3. 3. Drain and chill
    At After pour · 30s
    The coffee drips onto the ice and chills rapidly (about 30 seconds); gently swirl the server when done

Full method guide

Origin

Flash Brew (rapid-chill brewing) extends Japanese-style iced coffee: brewing hot coffee directly over ice for rapid cooling.

Core idea

Core principle: replace 40% of the total water with ice and brew a concentrate with only 60% hot water; the coffee dilutes back to normal strength as it drips onto the ice. Rapid chilling preserves more floral and fruit aromatics. The 1:15 ratio counts the ice as part of the total liquid.

Key points

  • Replace 40% of the total water with ice, placed in the server — weigh the ice, and never put it in the dripper or on the bed (it chills the grounds and under-extracts)
  • A second common school uses 50:50 (half hot water, half ice, about 1:16 overall): lighter and colder after dilution, great in real heat; the 60:40 default here is more concentrated with more aromatic focus
  • Brew with the remaining 60% as hot water (96°C, as hot as possible)
  • Grind one step finer than a normal pour-over to offset the under-extraction from less water
  • Bloom with 2x the dose for a fixed 30 seconds (common practice is 30-45s)
  • After brewing, gently swirl the server so the remaining ice melts evenly

Beans it suits

The florals and fruit of washed light roasts are stunning in flash brew; berry-toned natural light roasts also work well

Less suited to

Dark roasts (chilling makes the bitterness stand out more)

FAQ

Too weak/watery
Increase the dose (e.g. 1:13) or cut the ice share to 35%
Too sour/sharp
Grind another half step finer or extend the main pour
Ice does not fully melt
Normal - keep it as ice for serving
Can I use 50:50
Yes — that is the other common school: half hot water, half ice, about 1:16 overall. It comes out lighter and colder than the 60:40 default here; if it tastes watery, go back to 60:40 or raise the dose
Can the ice go in the dripper
No. Ice belongs in the server only. On the bed it chills the grounds and under-extracts badly — the most common cause of a failed flash brew
Not cold enough
Use solid cubes rather than crushed ice for better chilling
