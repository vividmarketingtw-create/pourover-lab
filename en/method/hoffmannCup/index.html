Step by step — Recipe for 15g of coffee (15g / 251g)

Bloom + four equal pours · locked start times
  1. 1. Bloom (about 3.3× the dose)
    At 0:00 · Pour 50g · up to 50g · 45s
    Rinse the paper and preheat the dripper, discard the water, add the grounds and press a small well into the centre, then start the timer.
    Pour 50g of freshly boiled water in circles (the original uses 50g on 15g — more than a usual bloom, because a one-cup bed is thin and has to be fully wetted), give the dripper a gentle swirl so every particle is wet, and wait until 45s (original value, does not scale with dose).
    Grind reference: Feima 600N 3.5-4 (like caster sugar; Hoffmann says the 1-cup version can go "as fine as you can without bitterness", so half a step finer than the big-batch version is fine)
    Other grinders: Comandante ~21-24 clicks / 1Zpresso JX ~2.1-2.4 turns
    ℹ️ Original (Hoffmann 2022, "A Better 1 Cup V60"): 15g / 250g, freshly boiled water, 50g bloom for 45s, 50g pours at 0:45 / 1:10 / 1:30 / 1:50 (about 10s each), a gentle swirl after the last pour, drained by about 3:00. Water by roast: 100°C light, 96°C medium, about 90°C dark. Why a separate recipe: scaled down to one cup, the Ultimate technique's two big pours push the water level too high and agitate too much — four small pours are steadier.
  2. 2. Pour 1 · 50g
    At 0:45 · Pour 50g · up to 100g · 25s
    Start at 0:45, pour 50g in about 11s (about 4.5 g/s). Small circles in the centre, spout low, a thin steady stream — the whole point of the 1-cup recipe is that every pour is identical: same grams, same speed, same circle. Then wait until 1:10 for the next pour.
  3. 3. Pour 2 · 50g
    At 1:10 · Pour 50g · up to 150g · 20s
    Start at 1:10, same technique, 50g in about 11s. The four start times 0:45 / 1:10 / 1:30 / 1:50 are the original values and are locked on this site; wait until 1:30 for the next pour.
  4. 4. Pour 3 · 50g
    At 1:30 · Pour 50g · up to 200g · 20s
    Start at 1:30, same technique, 50g in about 11s. The four start times 0:45 / 1:10 / 1:30 / 1:50 are the original values and are locked on this site; wait until 1:50 for the next pour.
  5. 5. Pour 4 · 51g
    At 1:50 · Pour 51g · up to 251g · 11s
    Last pour, 51g in about 11s, to 251g total. Then give the dripper one gentle swirl to level the bed — the 1-cup version is not stirred; that swirl is all.
  6. 6. Gentle swirl
    At 2:01 · holding at 251g · 10s
    Right after the last pour, one small swirl to level the bed and knock grounds off the wall. Keep it small — this is levelling, not stirring.
  7. 7. Wait for drawdown
    At After the pour · holding at 251g · 45s
    Let it drain fully, about 2:56 in total.
    💡 The original 15g / 250g drains by about 3:00. Sour / too fast → grind half a step finer; bitter / too slow → half a step coarser. For 20g and up, switch to the Hoffmann Clean & Bright method (the 30g / 500g structure).

Full method guide

Origin

James Hoffmann's 2022 "A Better 1 Cup V60 Technique". In his own words, the Ultimate V60 (30g / 500g) does not shrink well to a single cup — two big pours push the water level too high and agitate too much — so he built this version instead: 15g / 250g, 50g bloom for 45s, then 50g pours at 0:45 / 1:10 / 1:30 / 1:50, a gentle swirl, drained by about 3:00.

Core idea

The core is "four identical pours": same grams, same speed, same circle, same start time. With every variable locked, the only thing left to train is your hand — which is exactly why it is the best beginner recipe for rhythm and a steady stream. Water by roast: 100°C light, 96°C medium, about 90°C dark.

Key points

  • Setup: rinse the paper, preheat the dripper, press a small well into the dry bed (same as the big-batch version)
  • Bloom with about 3.3× the dose (15g → 50g) — more than the usual 2×, because a one-cup bed is thin and needs the extra water to wet fully; gentle swirl, fixed 45s
  • Four pours starting at 0:45 / 1:10 / 1:30 / 1:50 (original values, locked on this site — they do not move with dose or flow setting), about 10s each
  • Small circles in the centre, spout low, a thin steady stream — the water level is low in a one-cup brew and a rushed pour carves channels
  • One gentle swirl after the last pour to level the bed; the 1-cup version is not stirred
  • Grind can go half a step finer than the big-batch version — his words: "as fine as you can without bitterness"
  • For 20g and up, switch to Hoffmann Clean & Bright (the 30g / 500g structure)

Beans it suits

Washed light roasts show the clean brightness best; medium roasts are solid too. Also the handiest single-cup recipe for testing a new bag

Less suited to

Brewing for two or more (above 20g the structure no longer fits — use the big-batch version); for dark roasts drop the water to about 90°C

FAQ

How is this different from Hoffmann Clean & Bright
Different structure: the big-batch version is bloom + two large pours + stir, designed for 30g / 500g; the 1-cup version is bloom + four small pours + swirl, designed for 15g / 250g. Dose decides which one you use, not preference
Sour / drains too fast
Grind half a step finer — he says the 1-cup version can go "as fine as you can without bitterness". Make sure the water is freshly boiled
Bitter / drains too slowly
Grind half a step coarser; for dark roasts drop the water to about 90°C
I cannot finish a pour in time
Each pour is only about 10 seconds and not many grams — decisiveness is the skill. If it really does not fit, this site stretches the gap automatically, but aim to land each pour inside its window
